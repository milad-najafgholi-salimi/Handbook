In computing, a **cookie** (also called an HTTP cookie, web cookie, or browser cookie) is a small piece of data that a website stores on a user’s device (computer, phone, tablet) when the user visits that site.

### Key points:

- **Purpose:** Cookies help websites remember information about you, such as login status, language preferences, shopping cart items, or browsing activity. This makes the user experience faster, more convenient, and more personalized.
    
- **How they work:** When you visit a website, the server sends a cookie to your browser. Your browser saves it and sends it back to the server with every subsequent request to that site. This lets the server recognize you (or at least your browser) across different pages or visits.
    
- **Types:**
	- **Session cookies** – temporary, deleted when you close your browser.
	    
	- **Persistent cookies** – remain on your device until they expire or are manually deleted.
	    
	- **First-party cookies** – set by the website you are directly visiting.
	    
	- **Third-party cookies** – set by domains other than the one you’re visiting (often used for tracking, advertising, and analytics).
- **Security & privacy:** Cookies are generally harmless text files, but they can be used for tracking your online behavior. Modern browsers let you manage, block, or delete cookies, and privacy regulations (like GDPR) require websites to ask for your consent before using non-essential cookies.

**In everyday language:** A cookie is like a tiny "memory note" that a website leaves in your browser so it can recognize you next time you come back.
